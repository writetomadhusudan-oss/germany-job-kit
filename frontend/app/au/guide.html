How Australian hiring actually works

Australian hiring is informal, recruiter-driven, and ATS-heavy. A huge share of IT roles goes through recruitment agencies (Hays, Michael Page, Robert Half, Talent, Paxus) — recruiters actively search Seek and LinkedIn, and a good recruiter relationship can beat cold applications. Keywords from the posting on your resume are the price of admission past the ATS.

The two realistic paths: (a) get employer sponsorship (the Skills in Demand visa, replacing the old 482) and come on a work visa; or (b) get permanent residency independently through SkillSelect (subclasses 189 / 190 / 491) and job-hunt as a PR — which makes you dramatically more attractive to employers, since there's no sponsorship cost. Many Indian IT professionals do (b) first, then (a) becomes easy.

Timelines: 2–6 weeks from application to offer is common in tech. Notice periods are short (2–4 weeks), so start dates move fast.

The Australian resume

  • 2–4 pages is normal — longer than European or North-American resumes. Reverse-chronological.
  • Never include: photo, age/date of birth, marital status, nationality, or religion. These trigger bias concerns and mark you as unfamiliar with Australian norms.
  • Structure: Contact info → Professional Summary (3–4 lines) → Key Skills (bulleted) → Professional Experience (achievements with metrics) → Education → Certifications.
  • State your work rights clearly — "Full Australian work rights" removes the employer's biggest doubt about foreign applicants. If you're on a PR or visa, name it.
  • Mirror the posting's keywords for ATS: if the ad says "CI/CD pipelines", don't write only "DevOps automation".
  • Use the Resume Builder — it enforces this format automatically.

Cover letters

  • 3–4 tight paragraphs, under one page — Australian managers are direct and time-poor.
  • Format: your contact block, date, hiring manager block, "Re:" line, "Dear Hiring Manager," (or the name), then intro → fit → value → close. "Kind regards" is the standard sign-off.
  • Lead with what you can do for them, not your life story. Quantify: "cut deployment time 40%" beats "experienced in deployments".
  • Government and large-corporate roles often want you to address selection criteria — a separate document with one paragraph per criterion.

Where to apply

  • Seek (seek.com.au) — the dominant Australian job board; most tech roles are listed here.
  • LinkedIn — widely used; recruiters search it proactively, so keep your profile complete.
  • Government jobs — state government portals (e.g. iworkfor.nsw.gov.au, careers.vic.gov.au) and APSjobs for federal roles.
  • Company career pages — banks (CBA, Westpac, ANZ), Atlassian, Canva, and the mining/energy sector hire heavily direct.
  • Recruiters — Hays, Michael Page, Robert Half, Talent, Paxus: register with 2–3 and keep in touch.

SkillSelect — the points game (2026 figures)

  • Australia's General Skilled Migration ranks candidates by points (minimum 65 to lodge an EOI). Invitation rounds invite the highest scorers per occupation.
  • Age: 25–32 gets the full 30 points; 33–39 gets 25; 40–44 gets 15; you must be under 45 at invitation.
  • English: competent (IELTS 6 each band) = 0; proficient (7) = 10; superior (8) = 20. Superior English is the single biggest lever.
  • Skilled employment: overseas experience up to 15 points, Australian experience up to 20 — combined cap of 20.
  • Qualifications: Bachelor/Master = 15, Doctorate = 20. Plus 5-point boosters: Australian study, regional study, Professional Year, NAATI community language.
  • Visas: 189 (independent, no sponsor), 190 (state-nominated, +5 points), 491 (regional provisional, +15 points).
  • Be realistic: ICT and engineering invitations in recent rounds needed ~85–95+ points (4 June 2026 round). A skills assessment (e.g. ACS for ICT) and a valid English test are mandatory before you can be invited.
  • Note: a 2026 points-test reform has been proposed but not enacted as of September 2026.
  • Use the SkillSelect Points Calculator to estimate your score.

Interviews

  • Expect 2–3 rounds: recruiter screen, hiring manager + technical round, final (often culture fit).
  • Australian interviews are casual in tone but serious on specifics — mate-ship and "culture fit" genuinely count. Be warm, not stiff.
  • Prepare STAR-format stories with numbers: situation, task, action, result. "I led a team of 4 to cut deployment time 40%" lands every time.
  • Salary is discussed early and openly — know the annual package range (including superannuation) for your role and city. Sydney and Melbourne pay most; the gap is shrinking for remote roles.
