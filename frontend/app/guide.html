How German hiring actually works

German hiring is formal and document-driven. A typical application ("Bewerbung") consists of a cover letter (Anschreiben), a tabular CV (Lebenslauf), and copies of your degrees and certificates (Zeugnisse). Recruiters spend about 30 seconds on the first scan — clarity beats creativity.

For non-EU applicants there are two realistic paths: (a) get a job offer first, then enter on an EU Blue Card or skilled-worker permit; or (b) enter on a Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card) and job-hunt from inside Germany for up to 12 months. Most Indian IT professionals take route (a) — remote interviewing is normal in tech.

Timelines are slow by Indian standards: 4–8 weeks from application to offer is common, and notice periods (see below) mean start dates are often 2–3 months out. Plan accordingly.

Lebenslauf — the German CV

  • Tabular, reverse-chronological, max 2 pages. One page only if you have under 3 years of experience.
  • Photo: optional. Once standard, now increasingly omitted (anti-discrimination law). A professional photo is still accepted — never a casual one.
  • Leave out: date of birth, marital status, religion, parents' names. These were traditional but are now discouraged and irrelevant.
  • Structure: Personal details → Profile summary (2–3 lines, optional but recommended for internationals) → Professional experience (with quantified achievements) → Education → Skills → Languages (always with CEFR levels: A1–C2) → Certifications.
  • Dates in MM/YYYY format, e.g. 06/2021 – present.
  • Quantify everything: "Led EC implementation for 5,000 employees; go-live 2 weeks early" beats "Responsible for implementation".
  • Use the CV Builder — it enforces this format automatically.

ATS rules — getting past the software

  • Single column. No two-column layouts, no text boxes, no tables used for layout.
  • Standard fonts only: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica. No graphics, icons, charts, or photos embedded in the CV body.
  • Real headings ("Professional Experience", not "My Journey"). Mirror keywords from the job ad — the ATS matches literally.
  • File format: PDF, named Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf.
  • No headers/footers for critical info — some parsers skip them. Keep contact details in the body.

Anschreiben — the cover letter

Max one page, German business-letter format. Even when written in English (fine for IT roles), keep the formal structure:

  1. Your address block, then the company's address.
  2. Place and date, right-aligned.
  3. Subject line (Betreff): "Application for the position of X".
  4. Salutation: "Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren," — or better, "Sehr geehrte Frau Schneider," if you have a name.
  5. Paragraph 1: which role, where you saw the ad.
  6. Paragraph 2: why this company (one specific, researched reason).
  7. Paragraph 3: your 2–3 strongest qualifications, quantified.
  8. The German specifics: salary expectation (Gehaltsvorstellung, gross €/year), earliest start date (frühestmöglicher Eintrittstermin), and notice period. Recruiters expect these — omitting them looks evasive.
  9. Close with "Mit freundlichen Grüßen" and your name.

LinkedIn + Xing checklist

  • Xing is still used by German recruiters — create a profile; it takes 20 minutes.
  • Headline: target role + core skill, e.g. "SAP SuccessFactors Consultant | Employee Central & Recruiting | Open to Germany".
  • Location: set to "Germany" (or your target city) once you're seriously applying — many recruiters filter by location.
  • "Open to work" set to Germany, with the note that you require visa sponsorship (honesty saves everyone time).
  • Professional photo, quantified About section, skills endorsements for your top 5 skills.
  • Follow target companies; engage with their posts for 2 weeks before applying — warm applications convert better.

Where to apply

  • LinkedIn Jobs — highest volume for English-speaking IT roles.
  • StepStone.de — Germany's biggest general job board.
  • Indeed.de — strong aggregator coverage.
  • Honeypot / Welcome to the Jungle — tech-focused, startup-friendly, often English-first.
  • Company career pages directly — many German Mittelstand companies post only on their own sites.
  • Recruiters: Hays, Robert Half, Michael Page Germany all run English-speaking IT desks.
  • Apply on the company site when possible — direct applications are taken more seriously than "Easy Apply" blasts. Quality over quantity: 5 tailored applications beat 50 generic ones.

Visa routes — 2026 overview

EU Blue Card (§ 18g)

The fastest route if you have a job offer. 2026 thresholds: €50,700 gross/year standard; €45,934.20 for shortage occupations (all IT roles), recent graduates (degree within 3 years), and IT specialists with 3+ years experience in the last 7 (no degree needed in that case). Requirements: recognized degree (Anabin/ZAB) or the IT-experience rule, plus a 6+ month contract.

No German required. Permanent residence after 21 months with B1 German, 27 months with A1. Family can join from day one. Use the Blue Card Checker.

Chancenkarte — Opportunity Card (§ 20a)

Points-based job-seeker residence: live in Germany up to 12 months and search locally, no offer needed. You need 6 points (qualification, experience, language, age, ties to Germany), plus minimum German A1 or English B2, plus ~€13,092 in a blocked account. You may work up to 20 hrs/week during the search. Use the Points Calculator.

Skilled worker permit

The fallback: recognized degree or vocational training, job offer at local market salary. Slower path to permanent residence (typically 3–5 years) but no fixed EU salary threshold.

Interview norms in Germany

  • Stages: recruiter screen → hiring-manager interview → technical/case round → (sometimes) team lunch or trial day → offer. 3–4 rounds is normal.
  • Probezeit (probation): usually 6 months, with a 2-week notice period on both sides. Standard — not a red flag.
  • Notice periods (Kündigungsfrist) after probation are typically 3 months to quarter-end. This is why start dates are far out — state yours clearly.
  • Salary: quoted as gross annual. Ask about 13th salary (Weihnachtsgeld), vacation days (~30 is standard), and remote-work policy. Negotiating is expected; 5–10% above the first offer is a normal landing zone.
  • Punctuality and directness are cultural defaults — join calls 2 minutes early, answer questions directly, and send a short thank-you email after each round.

Pre-send checklist

  • ☐ CV is 1–2 pages, tabular, PDF, ATS-safe (run through the CV Builder)
  • ☐ Cover letter is one page, addressed to a person if possible, with salary expectation + start date
  • ☐ Degree certificates + transcripts ready as PDFs (you'll be asked)
  • ☐ Degree recognition started (Anabin check / ZAB statement) — it takes weeks
  • ☐ LinkedIn + Xing updated, location set to Germany
