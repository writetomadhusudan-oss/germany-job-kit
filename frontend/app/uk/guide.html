How UK hiring actually works

UK hiring is fast, recruiter-driven and ATS-heavy. For IT roles, recruitment agencies place a huge share of hires — being on good terms with two or three specialist tech recruiters can matter as much as your applications. Large employers and the public sector filter through applicant tracking systems, so keywords from the job posting in your CV are essential.

The two realistic paths: (a) land a sponsored job offer from a licensed Home Office sponsor and arrive on a Skilled Worker visa; or (b) come via another route (e.g. Global Talent, a UK degree) and job-hunt with the right to work already in hand — which removes the employer's sponsorship question entirely.

Timelines: 2–4 weeks from application to offer is normal in tech. UK notice periods are typically 1–3 months, so employers often plan start dates well ahead.

The UK CV

  • Maximum two pages — recruiters skim. One page is fine under 5 years of experience; two pages for senior profiles.
  • Never include: photo, age/date of birth, marital status, or nationality. These are irrelevant and mark you as unfamiliar with UK norms.
  • Structure: Contact info → Personal Statement (3–4 concise lines) → Key Skills → Work Experience (reverse-chronological, achievements with numbers) → Education → Certifications → "References available on request".
  • Use British spelling throughout (organised, prioritise, programme for training). It signals attention to detail.
  • Mirror the posting's keywords for ATS: if the ad says "CI/CD pipelines", don't write only "DevOps automation".
  • Use the CV Builder — it enforces this format automatically.

Cover letters

  • Concise — 3–4 short paragraphs, never more than one page.
  • Format: your address, date, recipient block, "Dear Ms Lee," (or "Dear Sir or Madam," if unknown), then why-this-role → your fit → close. Sign off "Yours sincerely" when you know the name, "Yours faithfully" when you don't.
  • Lead with what you can do for them. Quantify: "cut deployment time 40%" beats "experienced in deployments".

Where to apply

  • LinkedIn — the dominant channel for UK tech hiring.
  • Indeed UK — highest volume across sectors.
  • Reed.co.uk — the big UK-native job board.
  • CWJobs — the specialist IT/tech board.
  • Company career pages — banks (Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds), consultancies, and the London tech scene hire heavily direct.
  • Recruiters — Hays, Robert Half, and specialist tech agencies place a large share of UK IT roles. Apply and get on their books.

Skilled Worker visa — the salary test (rules in force since 22 July 2025)

  • Standard test: the salary must be at least £41,700/year OR the occupation's "going rate" — whichever is HIGHER.
  • Discounted floor: £33,400/year — available only with a valid discount: new entrant (under 26, recent UK graduate, or working towards a recognised qualification — 70% of going rate, max 4 years), relevant STEM PhD (80%), relevant non-STEM PhD (90%, floor £37,500), or a job on the Immigration Salary List (floor £33,400 but the full going rate still applies).
  • Hourly floor: £17.13/hour on guaranteed basic gross pay — bonuses, equity and overtime do not count.
  • Skill level: the job must be RQF 6 (degree level) or above since 22 July 2025.
  • English: B1 level required; some 2026 sources report B2 from 8 January 2026 — verify on gov.uk.
  • IT SOC codes (2134 programmers/software developers, 2133 IT business analysts, 2135 cybersecurity) are not on the Immigration Salary List — IT applicants normally use the standard £41,700/going-rate test or the new-entrant discount.
  • Use the Skilled Worker Checker to test an offer against these rules.
  • General information only — not legal advice. Thresholds change — always re-verify at gov.uk before applying.

Interviews

  • Expect 2–4 rounds: recruiter screen, hiring manager, technical assessment, sometimes a final culture-fit.
  • UK interviews lean on competency questions ("Give me an example of a time when…"). Prepare 5–6 STAR stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
  • Tone is professional but conversational — first names, measured pace, no hard sell. Understated confidence reads well.
  • Always prepare 2–3 questions for them; "no questions" reads as disinterest.
  • Salary expectations come up early — research ranges on LinkedIn Salary, Glassdoor and Reed before you quote a number.
