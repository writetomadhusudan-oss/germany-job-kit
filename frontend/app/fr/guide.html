How French hiring actually works

French hiring is formal and relationship-driven. A standard application ("candidature") is a CV plus a lettre de motivation — and in France the cover letter still genuinely gets read, especially at traditional companies and PME/ETI. Recruiters expect a clear, one-page, well-structured letter: vous (why this company), je (why you), nous (let's meet).

The realistic path for non-EU applicants: get a job offer first, then enter on a Passeport Talent or EU Blue Card. France has no direct equivalent of Germany's Chancenkarte — there is a "recherche d'emploi / création d'entreprise" card for certain graduates, but for most Indian IT professionals the offer-first route is the practical one. Remote interviewing is standard in tech.

Timelines: 3–6 weeks from application to offer is typical. August is a dead month (les grandes vacances) — avoid launching your search then if you can.

The French CV

  • One page is the norm (two only with 10+ years of experience). Reverse-chronological.
  • Photo: still common in France, though declining. A professional headshot is accepted and often expected — never a casual photo. The CV Builder lets you add one optionally.
  • État civil: name, address, phone, email, LinkedIn. Age and nationality are still frequently included in France (unlike Germany) — optional in the builder.
  • Structure: État civil → Profil (2–3 lines) → Expérience professionnelle (quantified achievements) → Formation → Compétences → Langues (with levels: courant, professionnel, etc.) → Centres d'intérêt.
  • Language honesty matters: if your French is A2, say so and show you're learning — recruiters check.
  • English-only CVs are accepted at international tech companies and startups; traditional companies expect French. When in doubt, send both.

Lettre de motivation

  • One page, formal business format: your details top-left, recipient top-right, place + date, "Objet : Candidature au poste de …".
  • Salutation: "Madame, Monsieur," if unknown; "Madame Dupont," / "Monsieur Martin," if you know the name.
  • The vous/je/nous structure: paragraph 1 — where you saw the ad; paragraph 2 (vous) — what attracts you to them; paragraph 3 (je) — what you bring; close with availability and a meeting request.
  • Closing formula: "Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées." Never shorten it.

Where to apply

  • Welcome to the Jungle — the go-to for French startups and tech.
  • LinkedIn — widely used; keep your profile in English and French if you can.
  • Indeed.fr, HelloWork — high volume, all sectors.
  • APEC (apec.fr) — the executive/professional job board, strong for experienced profiles.
  • Company career pages — French corporates (Capgemini, Atos, Sopra Steria, Thales, banks, luxury groups) hire heavily through their own portals.

Visa routes (2026 figures)

  • Passeport Talent — « salarié qualifié »: the main route. Needs a gross salary ≥ €39,582/year, a master-level diploma obtained in France, and a contract > 3 months. Valid up to 4 years; no employer work authorization needed.
  • Passeport Talent — « entreprise innovante » / « salarié en mission »: same €39,582 threshold, for innovative companies or intra-group transfers.
  • EU Blue Card (carte bleue européenne): gross salary ≥ €59,373/year, 3+ year higher-education diploma or 5 years relevant experience (3 years in the last 7 for shortage roles), contract ≥ 6 months.
  • Use the Passeport Talent Checker to test your profile against these thresholds.
  • General information only — not legal advice. Verify at service-public.gouv.fr (fiche F16922) and the French consulate.

Interviews

  • Expect 3–4 rounds: HR screen, hiring manager, technical/case round, final (sometimes with a director).
  • French interviews probe motivation for France and the company explicitly — "Pourquoi la France ? Pourquoi nous ?" Prepare a genuine answer.
  • Formality is higher than in Anglo-Saxon tech: punctuality is non-negotiable, dress one level up, use "vous" until invited otherwise.
  • Salary is usually discussed late; have your gross annual (brut annuel) expectation ready — French offers are always quoted in brut.
