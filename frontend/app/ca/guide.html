How Canadian hiring actually works

Canadian hiring is practical and ATS-heavy. Most large employers filter applications through applicant tracking systems before a human ever sees them — keywords from the job posting in your resume are not optional, they're the price of admission.

The two realistic paths: (a) get a job offer first (often with LMIA support from the employer) and come on a work permit; or (b) immigrate independently through Express Entry and job-hunt as a permanent resident — which makes you dramatically more attractive to employers, since there's no sponsorship paperwork. Many Indian IT professionals do (b) first, then (a) becomes easy.

Timelines: 2–5 weeks from application to offer is common in tech. Canadian notice periods are short (2 weeks typical), so start dates move fast.

The Canadian resume

  • 1–2 pages, reverse-chronological. Two pages is fine with 5+ years of experience.
  • Never include: photo, age/date of birth, marital status, nationality, or religion. These trigger bias concerns and mark you as unfamiliar with Canadian norms.
  • Structure: Contact info → Professional Summary (3–4 lines) → Highlights of Qualifications (bullet list of your strongest selling points) → Professional Experience (action verbs + quantified results) → Education → Technical Skills → Certifications.
  • State work authorization clearly — "Authorized to work in Canada" removes the employer's biggest doubt about foreign applicants.
  • Mirror the posting's keywords for ATS: if the ad says "CI/CD pipelines", don't write only "DevOps automation".
  • Use the Resume Builder — it enforces this format automatically.

Cover letters

  • Shorter than European letters — 3–4 tight paragraphs, under one page.
  • Format: your contact block, date, hiring manager block, "Re:" line, "Dear Hiring Manager," (or the name), then intro → fit → value → close.
  • Lead with what you can do for them, not your life story. Quantify: "cut deployment time 40%" beats "experienced in deployments".

Where to apply

  • LinkedIn — the dominant channel for Canadian tech hiring.
  • Indeed.ca — highest volume across all sectors.
  • Job Bank (jobbank.gc.ca) — the government's official board; useful and free.
  • Company career pages — banks (RBC, TD, Scotiabank), telcos (Bell, Rogers), Shopify, and the Toronto–Waterloo tech corridor hire heavily direct.
  • Recruiters — firms like Randstad, Hays, and Robert Half place a large share of Canadian IT contract roles.

Express Entry — the points game

  • Express Entry ranks candidates by CRS score (max 1,200). Draws invite the top scorers; recent cutoffs: ~515–523 (Canadian Experience Class), ~380–400 (French-proficiency draws), ~700–805 (PNP draws).
  • Biggest levers: language at CLB 9+ (boosts core points and unlocks transferability points), a provincial nomination (+600 — effectively guarantees selection), French at NCLC 7+ (+50 and access to low-cutoff French draws).
  • Age matters silently: full points at 20–29, declining yearly, zero at 45.
  • Foreign credentials need an ECA (educational credential assessment) from an approved agency, less than 5 years old.
  • A job offer adds 0 CRS points since March 2025 — but still helps with program eligibility and PNP streams.
  • Use the CRS Calculator to estimate your score.
  • General information only — not legal advice. Verify at canada.ca (IRCC).

Interviews

  • Expect 2–4 rounds: recruiter screen, hiring manager, technical/panel, sometimes a final culture-fit.
  • Canadian interviews lean heavily on behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time when…"). Prepare 5–6 STAR stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
  • Tone is friendly and informal — first names from the start. But keep answers structured and concise.
  • Always prepare 2–3 questions for them; "no questions" reads as disinterest.
